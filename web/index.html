    How to read this map

    1. Ask Volta. The assistant in the bottom right corner answers questions such as "Where near Albany can I connect 2 MW?" It sets the map, and every number comes from the data.
    2. Read the colors. Each colored line is a real distribution line. Its color shows the hosting capacity: how many megawatts of new solar can connect there without upgrades. Yellow has the most room, dark purple the least.
    3. Filter by project size. Set a minimum hosting capacity to hide lines too small for your project.
    4. Check a site. Enter your project size, then pick a spot on the map or search an address. The map finds the nearest line with enough room and shows how far it is.
    5. Share or save. A site check's card copies a link that reopens the same check. Zoomed in, the Download section saves the lines in view as a spreadsheet or a map file.
    6. Select a line. Zoomed out, a click selects the whole feeder and shows its capacity range. Zoomed in, a click selects one section and shows its exact value.
    7. Check data quality. Turn on the highlight to see where the utility's data is missing, outdated or inconsistent before relying on it.
    8. See what changed. Each week is compared with the one before. Turn on the change highlight to see where capacity went up or down, for example after new solar was connected.

    Terms

    Hosting capacity
    The amount of new generation, in megawatts (MW), a line can accept without upgrades. Published by each utility.
    Feeder
    One circuit leaving a substation, with all its branches. Projects on the same feeder share its capacity.
    Section
    A short stretch of a feeder, typically 100 to 500 ft in towns. Utilities publish a value for each one.
    Substation
    Where a feeder starts. It connects local lines to the high-voltage grid.
    kV
    Line voltage in kilovolts.
    DER
    Distributed energy resources, such as rooftop and community solar.

    Values are utility estimates for early screening. A utility interconnection study is still required for any project.
